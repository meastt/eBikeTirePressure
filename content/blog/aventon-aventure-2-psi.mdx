## Overview

The Aventon Aventure.2 features massive 26×4.0" fat tires designed for multi-terrain riding. With a 20-30 PSI range, these tires offer incredible versatility—from efficient pavement cruising to sand and snow flotation.

**Stock tire specs:**
- **Size:** 26×4.0"
- **PSI range:** 20-30 PSI
- **Casing:** Tubed (upgrade available)
- **Bike weight:** 77 lbs

## PSI by Terrain & Weight

### Pavement (Street/Bike Path)

| Rider Weight | Front PSI | Rear PSI | Feel |
|--------------|-----------|----------|------|
| 140 lbs | 24 | 26 | Cushioned |
| 180 lbs | 26 | 28 | Balanced |
| 220 lbs | 28 | 30 | Firm |
| 260 lbs | 29 | 30 | Max efficiency |

Higher PSI on pavement reduces rolling resistance and improves range. Expect 5-10% better efficiency at upper range vs. lower.

### Mixed Terrain (Pavement + Gravel)

Drop 3-4 PSI from pavement baseline:
- **180 lbs:** 23F/25R
- **220 lbs:** 25F/27R

This gives better traction on gravel while maintaining reasonable efficiency on paved sections.

### Dirt Trails (Hardpack)

Drop to 22-26 PSI total range:
- **Light (140-160 lbs):** 21F/23R
- **Average (180-200 lbs):** 22F/24R
- **Heavy (220-260 lbs):** 24F/26R

Lower pressure improves grip on loose dirt and roots without excessive squirm.

### Sand & Beach

Drop to near-minimum (20-22 PSI):
- **All weights:** 20F/22R to 21F/23R

Fat tires need low pressure for sand flotation. Expect sluggish handling but maximum traction.

**Pro tip:** Reinflate to pavement pressure before leaving beach to avoid rim damage on parking lot.

### Snow & Ice

Similar to sand, go low:
- **Packed snow:** 21F/23R
- **Powder:** 20F/22R (tire minimum)

Lower pressure increases contact patch for better traction. Use studded tires for ice.

## Cargo & Accessories

The Aventure.2 has a 300 lb payload capacity. Adjust rear pressure for heavy loads:

| Added Weight | Rear PSI Adjustment |
|--------------|---------------------|
| 20 lbs (groceries) | +1 PSI |
| 40 lbs (panniers) | +2 PSI |
| 60 lbs (camping gear) | +3 PSI |

Front pressure typically unchanged unless using front rack/basket (add +1 PSI per 20 lbs).

## Tubeless Conversion Benefits

Converting to tubeless allows:
- **1-2 PSI lower** for same rim protection
- Reduced pinch flat risk
- Sealant handles small punctures automatically

Example: 180 lb rider can run 24F/26R tubeless vs. 26F/28R tubed on pavement.

## Seasonal Adjustments

### Summer (80-100°F)
Start 1 PSI lower; heat increases pressure 1-2 PSI during ride.

### Winter (20-40°F)
Start 1 PSI higher; cold reduces pressure 1-2 PSI. Check before every ride.

### Temp swings >30°F
Recheck pressure when seasons change. 5 PSI drop is common from summer to winter.

## Performance vs. Comfort Trade-offs

### High PSI (28-30)
- ✅ Better range (5-10% gain)
- ✅ Faster on pavement
- ✅ Less motor power needed
- ❌ Harsher ride
- ❌ Less traction on loose surfaces

### Low PSI (20-22)
- ✅ Maximum traction
- ✅ Cushioned ride
- ✅ Better on sand/snow
- ❌ Reduced range (10-15% loss)
- ❌ Sluggish handling
- ❌ Higher pinch flat risk

### Sweet Spot (24-26)
Most riders, most terrain. Balanced efficiency and comfort.

## Common Issues

### Tire Squirm in Corners
**Cause:** PSI too low for rider weight  
**Fix:** Increase 2 PSI front and rear

### Harsh Ride on Pavement
**Cause:** PSI too high  
**Fix:** Drop 2-3 PSI without going below 24 for 180+ lb riders

### Frequent Flats on Trails
**Cause:** PSI too low, rim strikes  
**Fix:** Increase 1-2 PSI or add tire liners

## Gauge Recommendations

Fat tires at low PSI require accurate gauges:
- ❌ Gas station air (inaccurate below 30 PSI)
- ❌ Cheap analog gauges (±3 PSI error)
- ✅ Digital tire gauge (0.1 PSI precision)
- ✅ Floor pump with gauge (good for home)

## Maintenance Schedule

- **Check pressure:** Before every ride (fat tires lose 1-2 PSI/week)
- **Visual inspection:** Weekly (look for cracks, embedded debris)
- **Deep clean:** Monthly (remove thorns, check valve stems)
- **Tire replacement:** 1,500-2,500 miles depending on terrain
